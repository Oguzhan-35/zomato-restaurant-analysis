# Findings and recommendations

Numbers refer to [03-analysis-results.md](03-analysis-results.md).

## 1. Start with the 430 leads
Q7 finds 430 Indian restaurants with rating >= 4.0, at least 100 votes and no online delivery (average rating 4.24, average 798 votes). They already have customers and a good reputation, so the pitch is "you're missing a sales channel", not "you need more attention".

Suggested order: New Delhi (143) and Gurgaon (33) first because the team can cover them without travel. Then Lucknow, Goa, Guwahati and Pune (11-15 each) one city at a time, since sales effort per city has a fixed cost.

Caveat: I don't know why these restaurants aren't on delivery. Some may be dine-in only on purpose, which is why table booking and price range should be checked before the first call.

## 2. Use "unrated" as a second lead list
In India, 33% of restaurants without delivery have no rating, against 4% of those with delivery (Q9). That's 2,000 restaurants with no visible reputation. I can't tell from this data whether delivery causes ratings, or whether restaurants that care about ratings also join delivery. Either way, the sales message "orders bring reviews" is plausible but should be tested, not assumed.

## 3. Tailor the pitch by price range
Q4 shows two very different segments. Moderate-price places have the highest delivery adoption (45%) and almost no table booking. Upscale and fine-dining places mostly offer table booking (56-65%) and rarely delivery (11% for fine dining). A single pitch for both would be wrong. For upscale places it may make more sense to talk about packaging and brand rather than speed.

## 4. Do not expect delivery to raise ratings
Q3: 3.37 with delivery, 3.34 without. If the platform advertises "better ratings", the data doesn't support it. What the data does support is more votes (218 vs 162 on average), but I can't separate cause from selection.

## 5. Look at the small, well-rated cuisines
Q6 shows Mediterranean (3.98 average, 812 votes per restaurant), Sushi, Kerala and Seafood well above the big three (North Indian, Chinese, Fast Food at about 3.3). If the category team wants variety on the platform, these are where supply is thin and customer satisfaction is high. Caveat: they probably cost more, and higher price goes with higher ratings in Q4.

## 6. Attention is lopsided
65% of all votes belong to the top 10% of restaurants (Q8). Any ranking on the platform that follows votes will reinforce that. If the platform wants to surface newer places, it needs a signal that isn't votes.

## What I would want next
- Order-level data to measure real demand instead of votes.
- Dates, to separate new restaurants from unpopular ones.
- A reliable delivery flag for countries outside India.
- Confirmation from the sales team on whether the lead definition (4.0+, 100+ votes) matches what they consider a good lead.
