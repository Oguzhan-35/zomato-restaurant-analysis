# Business brief

This is the framing I wrote before touching the data. The scenario is invented.

## Situation
A food-delivery company's partnerships team signs restaurants onto its platform. There are many more restaurants than salespeople, so the order in which they are approached matters.

## Questions
1. What does the market look like: how many restaurants, where, how well rated?
2. Does being on online delivery relate to rating, popularity or price?
3. How does price range change what a restaurant offers?
4. Which cuisines are crowded and which are small but well liked?
5. Which restaurants are good, popular and not yet on delivery?

## Who would use the answers
| Person | What they need |
|---|---|
| Head of partnerships | which cities / segments to prioritise |
| Sales team | a concrete lead list with a reason for each |
| Category manager | which cuisines to promote or fill gaps in |
| Analyst (me) | numbers that can be reproduced |

## Measures used
| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| % rated | restaurants with rating > 0 / all restaurants |
| Average rating | mean of rated restaurants only |
| Delivery penetration | restaurants with online delivery / all restaurants |
| Votes | total number of user votes (used as a popularity proxy) |
| Lead | India, rating >= 4.0, votes >= 100, no online delivery |

## Out of scope
Revenue, order volumes, competitor platforms, anything with dates. The dataset has none of it.
